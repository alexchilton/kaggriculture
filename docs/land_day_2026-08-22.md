## What the arms actually did

`kagg/search/tape_edit.py:add_land` is the whole edit:

```python
out[step] = (farmer, hands, ([["BUY_LAND"]] + list(market))[:MAX_ORDERS])
```

One `BUY_LAND` order inserted at a chosen day and hour. Nothing else changes — no planting rows for
the new 25 tiles, no extra hires to work them, no seed bought for them.

And the new ground is unreachable by construction. On a replayed turn the unit actions are the
tape's verbatim (`schedule_agent.py:5789`), so every hand walks the route the q3 tape walked, on the
75 tiles the q3 tape used. No hand ever enters the fourth quadrant.

`LAND_PRICES = [1000, 2000, 4000]`, so the fourth parcel is **$4,000** spent on ground that is
provably never touched.

**So this is arithmetic, not a result.** Spend $4,000, gain nothing, lose about $4,000 and the games
that were close. It did not need 6,048 seasons.

## What the real question is, and how to ask it

*Is a fourth parcel worth $4,000 if you actually farm it?*

That needs an edit that does all of:

1. `BUY_LAND` on a day the bank can carry it — the q3 tapes hold $16,403 and $16,162 at d11 h0 and
   never dip below $12,385 afterwards, so the cash is genuinely there.
2. **Planting rows for the new quadrant**, which means editing the schedule, not just the market
   order.
3. **Hires** to work the extra 25 tiles, and the wage bill for the rest of the season.
4. Unit actions that visit the new ground — which the replay currently forbids, since it sends the
   tape's hands verbatim. This is the hard part and it is the reason the cheap version was tried
   first.

Point 4 is the real obstacle. Everything our code owns today is a *quantity* or a *label* on a turn
the tape chose — the animal species re-label, the `BUY_SEED` count, the carrot tile draws. Sending a
hand somewhere the tape never went is a different kind of edit, and it desynchronises every route
after it.

## What is genuinely settled

Only this: **an unaccompanied `BUY_LAND` is a $4,000 loss.** Do not add one on its own.

The q3-versus-q4 gap that motivated the idea is still open and still real — when `(True, True)` was
repointed at a four-quadrant recording it was worth about +$20k a game, and that was a tape that
farms its fourth quadrant, not one that merely owns it.

## Method note, which stands

`scripts/land_grid_verdict.py` restricts to contested seasons and scores on win rate. It was
validated against `tape_shootout_rows_2026-08-22b.csv` before use and reproduces that shootout's
published figures exactly — 90.0% vs 62.1%, +27.9, -$822 over 420 contested seasons. The tool was
right; the question put to it was not.
