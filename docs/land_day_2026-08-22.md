Restricted to the seasons each arm actually changed against control — a cell counts as changed when
its bank differs, which is exact, since the sim is deterministic given a seed.

**Verdict: do not move the land day.**
Control wins every comparison.
The best alternative, `land_d14`, is -12.3 points over 888 contested seasons and also banks $2,743
less, so there is no win-rate against bank tension to argue about.
The worst, `land_d16`, is -34.9.

`land_d14`'s contested wins by band, for completeness:

    low      144 contested    41.7%
    mid      228 contested    53.5%
    mirror   144 contested    14.6%
    top      372 contested    45.4%

The mirror column is the tell: 14.6%. Against a copy of the control agent, moving the land day loses
six games in seven. Buying the parcel on a different day is not a small perturbation — the whole
opening is priced around when that bank is spent.

## Why this was worth 6,048 seasons

The land day is one of the few things a replayed season could plausibly own without breaking the
tape: the parcel is bought with money, not with a unit action, so moving it does not desynchronise
the routes the way a changed unit op would.
It was a reasonable thing to test and the answer is a clean no.

## Method note

This is the same method as the tape shootout, and it is the method that matters more than the
result. Over all 6,048 rows rather than the contested subset, `land_d14` would read as roughly a
2-point loss instead of 12 — most cells play out identically under every arm, because the land day
only bites when the bank is near the parcel price on that day. Averaging over the untouched cells
dilutes a real effect by however many seasons were never in play.

`scripts/land_grid_verdict.py` was validated against `tape_shootout_rows_2026-08-22b.csv` before
being pointed at this, and reproduces that shootout's published figures exactly — 90.0% vs 62.1%,
+27.9, -$822 over 420 contested seasons.
