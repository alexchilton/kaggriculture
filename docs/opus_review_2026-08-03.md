## Verified, and it retracts my P1 refutation

**`terminal_loss_shares` does not measure what I said it measures.** It is fed
`sq_err = (ret_d - val_d) ** 2` (`train_jax_rl.py:2137`), and

- `returns = advantages + traj["value"]` (`:673`), `ret_d = batch_data_full["returns"]` (`:2099`)
- `val_d = flatten(traj["value"])` (`:2100`)
- `--target-ema` defaults to 0, so `target_params = params` and the rollout value *is* the current
  critic's value

Therefore `ret_d - val_d` is **identically `flatten(advantages)`**, and the metric reports the share
of squared **GAE advantage** carried by terminal rows — not the critic's value error.

Two consequences, both fatal to the conclusion I drew:

1. **The number is largely generated by the GAE recursion, not by the critic.** At gamma 1.0,
   `lambda = 0.95`, zero off-terminal reward and a smooth critic, `adv_{T-j} ~ 0.95^j * delta_T`, so
   squared error decays as `0.9025^j`. Summed over an 8-row window that predicts
   `near8 / term = (1 - 0.9025^8) / (1 - 0.9025) = 5.74`. Measured: **5.0-5.3**. So `near8 = 66-74%`
   is not independent evidence of anything; it is `term` multiplied by a constant computable from
   `--gae-lambda` alone.
2. **Loss share scales as `err^2`; gradient share scales as `err`.** `d(value_loss)/dV = 2(V - R)`.
   A per-row squared-error ratio of 88 is a per-row *gradient* ratio of `sqrt(88) = 9.4`, and terminal
   rows' share of summed gradient magnitude is `0.0014 * 9.4 / (0.0014 * 9.4 + 0.9986) ~ 1.3%`, not
   12%. My headline overstated it ninefold.

And the metric is close to circular: the ~99% of rows with near-zero error are near-zero *by bootstrap
self-consistency*, which is the pathology under investigation. A flat self-consistent critic drives
`lev` toward infinity; a well-calibrated one drives it toward 1. **`lev` is a symptom of terminal
blindness, not a measurement of gradient supply**, so "the critic receives the signal and fails to use
it" does not follow from it. **P1 is not refuted. It is unmeasured.**

The non-circular replacement, which also tests the value-clipping hypothesis for free: one extra
forward pass after `run_epochs`, reporting `mean|V_after - V_before|` on terminal rows against all
trainable rows, plus the signed mean on terminal rows split by `R=0` and `R=1`. Bit-identical
training. If terminal rows move ~0.2 the clip is binding; if they move ~0.001 while every other row
moves ~0.001, the gradient is being drowned and P1 is live.

## Verified: `ev` and `ev_signal` do not have a gap to explain

Since `ret_d - val_d` is identically the advantage, `ev = 1 - var(adv)/var(ret)` is approximately
`var(V) / (var(V) + var(adv))` — a smoothness statistic about the critic's own output against its own
TD residual. It cannot fail the way an explained variance can. The two numbers measure different
things; there is no convergence to wait for.

The consequence matters: **`CLAUDE.md`'s committed kill criterion — explained variance must pass 0.5
early — has to be read against `ev_signal`, not `ev`.** Read that way this run is *failing* it at
0.20-0.35 while `ev` reads 0.86 and looks like a pass. That is the "correct per row, meaningless
label" failure `CLAUDE.md` already warns about.

And `ev_signal` is computed over `done_d` — about **22 rows**. Its sampling standard error is of order
0.2-0.3, so the per-update 0.254 → 0.343 → 0.309 movement is white noise. The 44-update trend from
0.02 to ~0.31 is larger than that and is probably real, but the per-update series is not a detector.
Fix: accumulate `r_var`/`e_var` numerators and denominators and report a cumulative `ev_signal` over a
~200-update window. Four lines, and it is the difference between having a learning detector and not.

## Partly retracted: the value-clipping hypothesis

The gradient analysis is right. With `Vc = old_V + clip(d, ±c)` and the loss taking the `maximum`, a
step moving toward the target past the clip bound has **exactly zero** derivative, while an overshoot
keeps a live restoring force. So it is a hard per-update cap on movement toward the target — and worse
than I said, because `old_value` is a fixed field of `batch_data` (`:2084`), identical across both
epochs, so the cap is 0.2 *total* per update, not 2 x 0.2. Sigmoid saturation is not implicated at the
operating point (`dV/dz = V(1-V) = 0.113` at `V = 0.13`, against a 0.25 maximum).

But the conclusion does not hold. The clip caps a *step*, not cumulative learning, and the critic
generalises over ~440,000 terminal rows across a full run. The checkpoint at update 1800 spans
`V` 0.13-0.66, i.e. it has already moved further than 0.5 from initialisation; a binding 0.2/update
cap would have got there in three updates. At `--lr 3e-5` with Adam a single minibatch moves a scalar
output by ~1e-2, so the clip almost certainly never binds. The `ΔV` probe above settles it as a
by-product; do not spend a change on it first.

## Corrected: what gamma 1.0 actually buys, and what terminal blindness now means

The seat-sum argument is sound and there is no reading that saves `seat_loss` at gamma < 1. But:

- **The blast radius was overstated.** A level bias shared by both seats is symmetric by
  construction, so it corrupts the value *level*, not the seat *difference* — and the difference is
  what the policy consumes. Advantage normalisation (`:1043-1045`) removes most of the rest.
- **The strong argument for gamma 1.0 is the opposite of the one recorded.** At gamma 1.0 the
  bootstrap has no contraction, so any function constant along a trajectory is a fixed point (`:151-153`
  records this drifting to an impossible 1.315). The level is then anchored by exactly one thing —
  `seat_loss` — and the seat difference by exactly one thing, the 0.14% terminal rows. So gamma 1.0
  promotes `seat_loss` from mitigation to *the only level anchor*, and makes it exactly correct at the
  same time. That beats the `gamma^k`-target alternative, which fixes the constraint but leaves the
  drift problem `--n-phases` and the sigmoid were both added to contain.
- **Therefore one of the critic tests is now asserting the wrong thing.** At gamma 1.0,
  `V(s) = P(win | s)`, which for a decided game is 0 at *every* turn rather than decaying. So "the
  same lead at turn 0 versus turn 718" is no longer a discounting claim; the true day-dependence comes
  only from the lead becoming more *informative*. `V(hopeless, turn 718) = 0.139` remains
  unambiguously damning — it is a **calibration** failure, not a clock failure — but
  `test_the_same_lead_is_worth_more_at_the_bell_than_at_the_opening`'s `> 0.25` threshold is
  unjustified at gamma 1.0 and needs rewriting or removing.

## The structural fact that outranks all of the above

`--horizon 32` resets the GAE trace at `trunc` (`:663`), and at `--gae-lambda 0.95` the effective
reach is `1/(1-0.95) = 20` turns — under one game-day. **So the Monte-Carlo path from the terminal
reward can reach at most 32 turns back, and about 96% of an episode's actions can receive credit only
through the critic's `V_{t+1} - V_t`.** A flat critic zeroes the credit path for 96% of the game, and
the arithmetic above confirms it empirically: essentially all of the batch's squared advantage is one
terminal surprise plus its 20-turn echo, so on 99% of rows the normalised advantage is amplified
noise.

| reading | verdict |
|---|---|

*selection* criterion; `CLAUDE.md` forbids selecting on money, not measuring with it. Do not use
per-update `ev_signal` as the detector until it is cumulative.
