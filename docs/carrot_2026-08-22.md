## It survives the confound, and that is the part worth trusting

The town's drain budget is shared (`mean total 20.7, sd 1.9`), so a carrot-hungry town wants less of
something else by construction.
We are a wheat/strawberry farm, so the raw gap could be "the town does not want what we grow"
wearing a carrot label — the same correct-number-wrong-label trap as `farm:0.445`.

    corr(carrot, wheat+straw)  -0.256
    corr(carrot, total)        +0.166

The confound is real but small. Controlling for it:

    win%              ours-low          ours-high
    carrot-low     59.1% (n=252)     60.6% (n=315)
    carrot-high    49.0% (n=294)     45.6% (n=147)

- Across the rows: demand for **our** crops moves us **+1.5 points**. Nothing.
- Down the columns: carrot demand costs **-10.1 points** when the town wants little of ours, and
  **-15.0 points** when it wants a lot.

Carrot dominates, and the effect is *stronger* in towns that also want our crops — the opposite of
what a substitution artifact would do.

## What this adds to the settled rule

The conditional rule's strongest bucket is `town CA>=4`, and it rests on 3 towns / 57 seasons.
The drain table above is 48 towns / 9 opponents / 1,008 seasons and points the same way: the towns
where we lose are the carrot-hungry ones, and it is not a substitution artifact.
`town CA>=4` and `drain_CARROT >= 5` are the same population seen from two directions, so the two
measurements agree on a far wider base than either alone.

What it does **not** settle, and what the killed sweeps were for:

    carrot2_d25d27      two tiles in the window
    carrot4_d25d27      four tiles in the window        <- how many, at the known-good days
    x_carrot            the static swap
    cond_carrot_d4_o7   the runtime rule at town CA>=4, opp CA<=7   <- gate width

Nothing new needs designing. Re-run them, six seeds, and read the arms against each other rather
than against the question of whether carrot works.

## Caveats

- Win rate against the seated opponent, not Elo.
- 3 seeds; project seed variance is ~15%. A 15-point split on 819 seasons is outside that; the
  drain-6 row (57.8%, n=147) breaking the monotone trend is not explained.
- `drain_*` is town demand, not our fills. It says what the town would buy, not what we sold it.
