# Day 6: the two missing waters, found, tried, and reverted on measurement

Seed 11, kawashigi grid, weeds 0, recorded shop draw injected, measured on top of the d6 wool ferry fix (`b92e1bc`).
The d6 row reads `water 17/19`.
This documents where the two waters are, what the fix did, and why it is reverted.

## Where the two waters are

A per-tile probe of d6 (every WATER op attributed to the acting unit's tile, both seats) says the gap is entirely the ongoing crops:

- Both boards carry 19 established crop tiles at dawn d6: 3 wheat, 12 melon, 4 strawberry, all planted to the same schedule.
- The tape waters 17 of its 19 plus its 2 new plantings = 19.
- We water 15 of our 19 plus our 2 new plantings = 17.
- The four tiles we skip are our four strawberries: (2,3), (3,3), (0,4), (1,4).
- The tape skips only two of its strawberries: it waters 2 of 4, staggering the block half and half.

The mechanism is an asymmetry in `router.crop_ops`.
`WATER_ROTATION` — water half the SAFE tiles each day on `(x + y + day)` parity — was added to the ONE-SHOT branch on melon evidence, and only there.
The ongoing branch (strawberry, tomato) offers a safe tile nothing: survival fires at `consecutive_unwatered >= 1`, the production branch waits for `produces_tomorrow`, and `TOP_UP_SAFE_TILES` is measured OFF.
So our strawberries, all planted d5, swing 0/4/0 on alternate days off the survival rule, where the tape staggers 2/2.
On d6 the two even-parity strawberries (3,3) and (0,4) are exactly the two missing waters.

## The fix, and why it is reverted

The same parity clause was added to the ongoing branch's safe-tile slot, same top-up price (`_crop_remaining / TOP_UP_DISCOUNT`), with a red-first test (`test_a_safe_ongoing_tile_joins_the_water_rotation`, since deleted with the revert).
It did what it said at the op level — the probe shows Job(3,3) and Job(0,4) offered and toured — and it measured as a LOSS at the target day:

| d6 row | before | with rotation on ongoing |
|---|---|---|
| water | 17/19 | **17/19 — unchanged** |
| plant | 2/2 | **0/2** |
| melon (end of day) | 14/14 | **12/14** |
| sell / buy_seed / feed | 14/17, 15/15, 6/6 | 14/17, 15/15, 6/6 |

The route dump shows why the count did not move: `cover.plan` seated the two 1-turn strawberry waters and left `uncovered=[Job(7,4) PLANT, Job(5,3) PLANT]`.
The waters ate exactly the slack the two far PLANT jobs in the new quadrant needed — unit3 ended its water run at (3,3) instead of finishing at (5,3), and unit4 spent its spare walking to (0,4).
The two plants slid to d7 (`plant 11/9`), which is a d6 regression under days-match-in-order, and two waters for two plants is a bad trade the tape never makes.

The tape affords both rows because it has more day: 116 unit-turns to our 113.
Its hands arrive at h0-h1 where ours trickle in to h2, and three unit-turns is precisely the walk-plus-work the two plants cost.
Crew arrival is out of scope here and is the real constraint.

So per the standing rule the clause was reverted the same day and the test deleted with it; this document is the record, and the commit message names the null so it is not re-derived.
No note was left in `router.py` itself, deliberately: that file's `WATER_ROTATION` work is still uncommitted on the branch, and annotating it would have swept someone else's work-in-progress into this commit.
`water 17/19` on d6 stands as accepted-for-now: the two missing waters are safe-tile top-ups on strawberries that cannot weed tomorrow (both were watered on d5), while the two plants they displace are the schedule's row.

## What a future fix needs

- The finding about WHERE the waters go is solid and cheap to reuse: the rotation asymmetry between the one-shot and ongoing branches is real, and from d7 onward the rotation-on-ongoing grid actually tracked the tape's water column more closely (d11 39/39 and d12 40/40 exact, against 44/39 and 30/40 without it).
- Any retry must stop a top-up water from displacing scheduled work — the greedy's final pass seats both and ranks only on insertion cost, so a 1-turn nearby water always beats a 2-turn far plant.
- Or it must find the three unit-turns: the binding constraint on d6 is crew arrival, not willingness to water.
