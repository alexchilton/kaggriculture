## How it was found, because the method is the transferable part

**Three changes had to land before the cause was even visible**, and each one removed an explanation
that had been absorbing the blame.

### 1. The signal problem was real, and seeding fixed it

Every opponent returned a **constant** outcome: 0.000 against idle, 0.000 against the scripted
farmer, 0.060 against the trader, and 89% draws against a frozen self because two bankrupt farms tie
exactly. A constant carries no gradient at any model size or step count. That is why 1,300 updates of
the previous run went nowhere.

Seeding episodes from real recorded boards at game-day 25 (`kagg/rl/curriculum.py`) made episodes
about four game-days long and fixed it outright:

| | turn-0 runs | day-25 seeding |
|---|---|---|
| terminal games per update | 10-12 | **150-170** |
| bank median | $0 | $66,000 |
| terminal_ret_std | 0.0-0.4 | 0.49 |
| wr vs farmer | 0.000 for 1,300 updates | **0.301** |
| wr vs idle | 0.000 for 1,300 updates | **0.422** |

**The reward is untouched** -- still win/lose at the end of the season. What changed is where episodes
begin. That distinction is load-bearing: potential-based shaping is provably equivalent to
initialising the value function (Wiewiora 2003), so importing a hand-designed intermediate value is
the banned thing through another API, while changing the initial-state distribution is not -- the
agent still has to win from wherever it lands and is judged only on whether it did.

**Measured property that makes seeding work rather than merely help.** Seed at day 15 and take no
action at all:

| game-day | bank | crops | animals | weeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | $13,183 | 22.1 | 19.2 | 0.8 |
| 17 | $13,183 | 3.9 | 9.8 | 19.0 |
| 18 | $13,183 | **0.0** | **0.0** | 22.9 |

Three days of inaction destroys the farm -- crops die after two unwatered days, animals escape after
two unfed ones. **A seeded position cannot be held by standing still.** That is why this beats the
otherwise-attractive idea of adding a deliberately hopeless opponent: an opponent that ends at $0 is
beaten by a policy that does nothing, which rewards the exact attractor the project was stuck in.

**Short horizons are also what make FEED learnable at all.** At four game-days, feeding is causally
near-terminal -- feed the herd and you end richer, do not and it escapes -- so the terminal reward is
nearly a direct readout of whether you maintained the farm. No bonus required. At 719 turns the same
action is four steps and eight days from any signal.

### 2. With the signal fixed, the failure was still there — and had nowhere left to hide

The BC clone opens at **0.525** on a real board. Genuinely competitive. Eighty updates of PPO took it
to 0.281, monotonically, on six metrics at once:

| update | 0 | 24 | 48 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| entropy | **13.35** | 20.13 | 24.94 | **27.06** |
| win rate | **0.525** | 0.409 | 0.329 | **0.281** |
| feed | 3.47 | 2.37 | 1.0 | **0.61** |
| dig | 1.44 | 1.49 | 3.2 | **4.55** |
| bank median | $66,397 | $55,516 | $40,018 | **$33,202** |
| clip_frac | 0.250 | 0.310 | 0.227 | 0.211 |
| ev_signal | -0.031 | +0.134 | +0.238 | **+0.305** |

**Entropy doubles while the win rate halves.** And the same table excludes the alternatives, which is
what makes this a diagnosis rather than a story:

- **Not step size.** `clip_frac` 0.21-0.31, `approx_kl` flat at +0.02. `CLAUDE.md`'s own warning
  threshold is ~0.3 and it is touched only briefly.
- **Not the critic.** `ev_signal` climbs to **+0.305**, the highest ever recorded here (previous best
  0.178). The critic was learning well. It was getting better at predicting a policy getting worse.
- **Not the reward, exploration, or horizon.** All three changed; every signal metric improved.

### 3. The mechanism, and why it had been invisible

`regulariser = reg_coef * entropy_loss` (`scripts/train_jax_rl.py:736`) with `klP 0.000` on every
line. So the only fixed pull on the policy was toward **uniform over legal actions** -- and that
distribution *is* the failure mode. Measured: the policy's op choice correlates **0.854** with pure
legality-availability.

| op | legal on | |
|---|---|---|
| NOOP | **100%** | of live unit-turns |
| WEST / NORTH | 95% / 88% | |
| BUILD_PASTURE / COOP | 45% / 45% | |
| DIG | 42% | |
| **WATER** | **7.3%** | |
| **FEED** | **under 3%** | |

The useful ops are only legal at the *end* of a correct sequence -- walk to the tile, arrive, then
act. So pushing toward uniform-over-legal is pushing toward walking and digging. The dumb behaviour
was never a learned strategy; it is what a near-zero gradient leaves behind, filtered through a mask
whose shape favours doing nothing useful.

**Why raising `ent_coef` 6.7x had previously shown "no measurable effect".** That was measured on a
policy already at 95% NOOP -- already sitting at the entropy bonus's own fixed point, with nothing
left to push against. A competent policy is the opposite starting condition. Same lever, two very
different distances from its target. **A null result from a broken starting point is not evidence
the lever is harmless.**

## The two bugs in the fix itself

**`--kl-prior`'s help text said "Set --ent-coef 0 when using this".** `reg_coef = args.ent_coef` and
`regulariser = reg_coef * kl_prior`, so following that instruction gives **no regulariser at all**.
This is the identical error `HANDOFF.md` records as having already cost two runs via `--kl-coef` --
fixed there, and it survived here. It had never fired only because nobody had used `--kl-prior`.

**The curriculum's seed pool was built once and never rebuilt.** `fresh = seed_states` indexes
elementwise on restart, so slot *i* restarted into the *same board* every episode -- roughly nine
repeats of one recorded game per slot. `--curriculum-resample` (default 25) redraws it.

## The strategy-bias question, which is not settled

Seeding from the top seats by bank means seeding animal-heavy boards: of 48 day-25 boards, **46 carry
five or more animals, median 17**. That is a strategy choice embedded in the state distribution, and
it deserves to be named rather than defended.

**It matters because the animal economy is not the only way to play well.** Across 140 sampled seats:

```
  132,245  feed 502  care 498  plant  80  water  331    <- animal cluster
   86,262  feed  20  care  20  plant 162  water 1288    <- crop cluster
   86,206  feed   0  care   0  plant 163  water 1256    <- ZERO feeding, $86,206
```

30 of 124 seats above $60k are in the **bottom quartile** for feeding, and bank correlates with feed
count at only **0.227**. So at least two viable strategies exist and both clear $86k -- above
`contrarian`'s $51k and above the scripted farmer's $30k. Only the very top ($120k+) is animal-heavy.

**`HANDOFF.md`'s claim that "the animal economy is the game" is therefore too strong.** It holds for
the very top and for the op-share correlations; it misses a crop cluster that plays well.

`--curriculum-max-bank` varies which boards are drawn. Measured pools at day 25:

| band | animals (median) | boards with 0-4 animals |
|---|---|---|
| top by bank (current default) | 17 | 2/48 |
| $15-35k | **0** | 45/48 |
| $35-70k | 16 | **15/48 -- mixed** |

**But a board is a position, not a strategy, and that distinction kills the obvious hope here.** Day
25 is the *end* of the game. The strategic decision -- buy animals or plant crops, build pastures or
buy land -- was made by the original player around day 0-8, and by day 25 it is history. A policy
seeded there inherits the *consequence* of a strategy and can only maintain or squander it. Varying
the band varies which finished position it must hold; it does not hand the policy a choice.

So board diversity is worth having for generalisation, and it is now available -- but it does **not**
answer the bias objection. **It defers it.** The strategic decision only becomes live around day 0-8,
which is precisely where the schedule arrives last, with the least accumulated exposure, on weights
specialised for maintenance. Any claim that the policy is "choosing a strategy" has to wait until the
schedule actually reaches there, and nothing measured so far says it will.

## What is NOT yet known, stated so it is not assumed

- **Whether this generalises to turn 0.** The current policy trains only on turns 600-719. It has no
  experience of the opening, cannot play a real game, and is not submittable. `--curriculum-anneal`
  walks the day back, but walking it back means day 0 arrives *last*, with the least exposure, on
  weights specialised for maintenance. The failure mode to watch: at day 0 there are no animals, so
  FEED is illegal, and the policy may regress to the DIG/NOOP attractor exactly as before.
- **Whether backward chaining will carry credit.** It depends on the critic learning to value
  intermediate states. `ev_signal` at +0.31 is the best ever seen here but the committed kill
  criterion is 0.5, and the sharper probe measured V spanning only 0.396-0.629 on a 0/1 reward --
  all state-dependent information compressed into ~5% of the range. **Re-run that probe at each
  schedule step. If the gap between V(has animals) and V(no animals) grows, chaining is working. If
  it stays at +0.05, the schedule will regress and more days will not help.**
- **Whether BC is necessary at all.** Untested and it is the cheapest remaining experiment: a
  randomly-initialised net, same day-25 seeding, same opponents. If a raw net also starts near 0.5,
  the *seeding* is doing the work and BC is incidental. If it starts low and climbs while BC-init
  starts high and falls, BC init was a trap under the old regulariser. `jax_policy.py:126-129`
  zero-initialises the action heads, so a fresh net starts exactly uniform-over-legal -- which is
  the entropy bonus's own fixed point, so it would have had nothing to erode.
- **Whether the old turn-0 BC decay was entropy or covariate shift.** Both were plausibly operating
  and the runs on disk cannot separate them. The test is one run: turn-0 with `--kl-prior` and no
  other change. If the decay stops, entropy was the cause and `HANDOFF.md`'s "ordinary covariate
  shift" verdict on the parked BC track is wrong.

## Things established today that should not be relitigated

- **An opponent that earns from the shared market is not a stable rung.** The scripted trader banks
  $3,368 against an idle seat and **$2** against our policy, because our policy trades the same wheat
  and `_process_market` re-quotes after every unit. `wr_trade` reading 0.31 was two bankrupt agents
  comparing pocket change. A rung must earn from its own board.
- **The scripted farmer cannot hold an inherited herd.** `jax_heuristic` has no FEED, no CARE, and
  `buy_animal=zeros` (`:192`). Seeded at day 25 with 16 animals it loses all of them within four
  game-days, **32/32 games**. It is still a legitimate opponent -- its bank rises $61,440 -> $74,180
  over the same period from crops alone -- but **no opponent in the pool ever uses the animal
  economy**, so the trainee is repeatedly shown an opponent that discards 16 animals and wins anyway.
- **Overspending was never the diagnosis.** `--spend-prior` halved seed and hire rates and the bank
  did not move. The winners themselves end day 0 on $395 against our $85 -- they spend just as hard,
  they simply have 12.4 living crops to show for it.
- **A replay observation is the state AFTER its own action.** `iter_turns` had paired them at the same
  index, which made the teacher's own label illegal under the rebuilt masks on 100% of WATER (0 of
  14,009), PLANT (0 of 2,297) and FEED (0 of 9,097) rows. The legality gate then dropped them, so
  those ops could not be learned. Label legality went 65.2% -> 98.7%. **The earlier conclusion that
  "BC cannot teach FEED" was a data bug, not a fact.**
- **`PLANT` carries a crop argument.** `HANDOFF.md` states it does not. It does, in every replay, and
  the reference refuses a PLANT without one. Believing otherwise trains every PLANT toward crop 0,
  which is WHEAT.

## The mixed curriculum, which replaces the staged anneal as the plan

Pure backward annealing was the plan in this file's "Next, in order" below, and Alex's objection to it
is correct and structural: **it reaches day 0 last, with the least accumulated exposure, on weights
specialised for maintenance** -- an objection this file already recorded against the very schedule it
was proposing. A mix removes it by construction, and removes a second failure the anneal cannot: when
a staged schedule steps off day 25, that distribution is **gone from the data**. Re-anchoring a KL
prior preserves the behaviour partially; nothing preserves the data. In a mix nothing is ever removed.

`--curriculum-mix 25:0.8,0:0.2` seeds both days into one batch.

**The share is not what it looks like, and this is the number to hold on to.** Episodes are ~120
turns at day 25 and 719 at day 0, and every slot emits the same `--horizon` turns per update, so:

| day-0 slot share | day-0 share of terminal episodes |
|---|---|
| **20%** | **~4%** |
| 40% | ~10% |
| 60% | ~20% |

A day-0 episode also carries its reward across ~22 horizon segments instead of ~4, so it is weaker
per episode as well as rarer. **So a small day-0 fraction does not buy policy credit on the opening.
It buys coverage** -- opening states stay in the distribution, so the critic can learn to value them
and they never go out-of-distribution. That ordering is the point rather than a limitation: policy
gradient on an opening is worth nothing until the critic separates a good opening from a bad one, and
the mix does that critic work *concurrently* with the day-25 policy work at ~4% of the episodes,
where the anneal would do it serially and last. The trainer prints both shares on startup so the slot
share cannot be mistaken for the signal share.

**The composition is deliberately not annealable.** Its premise is that it shifts on the value probe
rather than the update counter, so a flag that walked it on the clock would reintroduce exactly what
it replaces. The trainer refuses `--curriculum-mix` with `--curriculum-anneal` and with
`--curriculum-day`. Shift the mix by relaunching with new fractions.

**The existing value probe cannot drive it, which is why there is a new one.** `value_vs_animals`
bins by animal count, and day 0-8 boards carry ~0 animals on both sides, so they all land in the same
"0 animals" bucket -- the animal gap can read healthy while the critic is blind to openings.
`diagnose_policy.py` section 4 groups V by game-day instead. **Read the spread within a day, not the
mean:** the mean rises with the day because later boards are richer and nearer a decided game, which
is the season progressing rather than the critic seeing. A flat spread across days 0-8 means those
slots generate no usable advantage at any fraction, so raising the day-0 share would cost the day-25
signal and buy nothing.

**Four flag combinations are now refused rather than documented,** because each produces a run that
looks fine and trains something other than what was asked: fractions that do not sum to 1; a day
whose fraction rounds to zero slots (an "80/20" run that logs 80/20 and trains pure day-25);
`--curriculum-mix` with `--curriculum-day` or `--curriculum-anneal`; and `--early-limit` non-zero,
checked against the **latest** day in the mix, since a day-25 slot truncates against a limit of 288
whatever the day-0 slots do and a check written against the minimum would pass while most of the
batch never finished an episode.

The seed pool is permuted so the game-day is not carried by the slot index. Seat-1 roles are drawn
i.i.d. per slot today, so block ordering is not currently a confound -- but a restarting slot restarts
into `seed_states[i]` **by position**, so any future per-slot assignment would inherit the block
structure invisibly.

shows up as `wr nan` and `tstd 0.000`, not as an error. The trainer now refuses the combination.

   One delta at a time -- it is not in the current run. Do not expect it to answer the strategy-bias

5. **The raw-net control.** No BC, everything else identical. Still the cheapest way to learn whether
   BC init does anything now that the regulariser is fixed, and still untested.
6. **Whether the old turn-0 BC decay was entropy or covariate shift** is now partly answered by
   construction -- the mix puts day-0 states in front of a KL-regularised policy from update 1. If the

**The staged ladder is the fallback, not the plan.** `scripts/stage_curriculum.py` implements it if the
mix does not carry: days 25/20/15/10/5/0, re-anchoring `--kl-prior` **and** `--init` to the previous

position (bank level with the update-0 seed value), a flat value gap, a non-positive `ev_signal`, and a
near-constant `terminal_ret_std`. Prefer the mix: staging forgets each stage's data as it leaves it.

### Two rules this session paid for

- **A null result measured from a broken starting point is not evidence the lever is harmless.**
  `ent_coef` raised 6.7x for "no measurable effect" -- on a policy already at the entropy bonus's own
  fixed point. The same lever was destroying a competent policy within 80 updates.
- **A board is a position, not a strategy.** Varying which day-25 boards get seeded varies which
  finished position must be held; it does not give the policy a strategic choice, because the choice
  was made around day 0-8 and is already spent by day 25. This was written up here as "the policy
  would be choosing a strategy" before Alex pointed out that it cannot be.
- **When a metric moves for the first time, measure the opponent before believing it.** `wr_trade`
  rising 0.06 -> 0.31 was written up here as the project's first real upward movement. It was our
  policy demolishing the market the trader depends on, and both agents finishing near $0.

- **Four flat points inside the noise band are not a plateau.** The day-25 run was called plateauing
  at ~0.59 on updates 64-100 spanning 0.012, then went to 0.6125 with `ev_signal` nearly doubling. The
  rule this file already stated -- judge on the bank curve, not the win rate -- would have got it
  right, and the bank was rising the whole time. Before calling a trend, check the metric's own
  update-to-update variation and check the metric the doc told you to use.

- **A share of slots is not a share of signal when episode lengths differ.** An 80/20 day-25/day-0 mix
  is ~96/4 in terminal episodes, because a day-0 episode is six times longer and every slot emits the
  same turns per update. Getting 20% of *episodes* from day 0 needs ~60% of *slots*. Any curriculum,
  replay buffer or sampling ratio quoted in slots should be restated in whatever unit the gradient
  actually consumes before it is reasoned about.

- **A probe can be correct and still be unable to see the thing you are about to gate on.**
  `value_vs_animals` bins by animal count; day 0-8 boards all carry ~0 animals and land in one bucket,
  so it would have read healthy while the critic was blind to openings -- and it was the number
  nominated to drive the mix. Before using an existing measurement for a new decision, check that the
  new decision's failure mode is inside its resolution.

- **A hand-launched run writes where the tooling does not read, and that is indistinguishable from a
  dead run.** `kagg-log` followed `data/logs/train.log`; the systemd service and
  `deploy_train.sh watch` use `run.log`. Which one worked depended on how the run was started. Launch
  through `deploy_train.sh train` -- it also avoids WSL2 reaping a session-owned process when the SSH
  connection closes, which `deploy_train.sh:92` records as having killed several runs while looking
  like a startup crash.
