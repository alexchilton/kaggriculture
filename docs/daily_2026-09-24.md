## Three findings worth keeping

**1. The interesting churn is in RE-RUNS, not new slugs.** `master_engine_v3`, `harvest-ledger`
and `the-2965-master-hybrid-engine` all re-ran overnight under the same URL with materially
different bytes - `master_engine_v3` grew by 205 KB. Yesterday's measurements of those slugs were
stale within a day. A sweep that only diffs slug names will miss this entirely; diff the .ipynb
sha256 as well.

**2. haodou092 moved onto the melons chassis and got WORSE.** Yesterday his build was byte-identical
to Guru Prasaath's and lost 24/80 to Pipe-19. Overnight he rebuilt on prvsiyan's chassis - same
entry point `_final_sell_block_reorder_entrypoint`, 7,340 lines against melons' 7,333, and he kept
`_ADV_LOOK=4`. It lost **13/80 at t=-8.00**, the worst result of the day. Taking a strong chassis
is not the same as keeping what made it strong.

**3. A notebook can declare a hash that is not its own agent.** `mengine_v2` declares exactly one
sha256, `bc8a54879ef02c7e...`, and that is the **kaggriculture engine source**, the same constant
that appears in Gluzdov's notebook as `EXPECTED_ENGINE_SHA256`. The author declares no hash for his
own `main.py`. That is unverifiable, not falsified - it may be measured, but it must not be
submitted, because there is nothing to check provenance against. Check WHAT a declared hash is for,
not just whether one exists.

Also: `evgendvorkin/kaggriculture` (59 votes) cannot be extracted here at all. Its cells read a
private `/kaggle/input/datasets/evgendvor...` path, so there is no self-contained agent in the
notebook.
