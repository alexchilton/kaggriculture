## THE HEADLINE: it learnt capital preservation, not production

Two full-season replays from a real $3,000 start, via
`scripts/play_checkpoint.py <ckpt> --opponent {farm,carrot,ranch,idle}`:

```
vs carrot ($4,448):   0/0/6    bank 3,000 -> 2,988   DEAD FLAT
vs farm  ($30,292):   0/0/12   bank d0:3,000 d10:2,790 d15:29 d20:0 d29:0
both:  ~65% movement, PLANT ~1%, WATER ~1%, HARV 0.06%, FEED fires ZERO times
```

Corroborated by the seed-day gradient — d25 0.62, d20 0.43, d15 0.40, d10 0.37, d0 0.36.
Frugality pays less the longer the horizon, which is exactly the curve a "don't spend" policy
produces. At d25 there are ~5 days left, nothing can mature, and holding cash *is* near-optimal;
at d0 it is fatal.

**These are TWO failure modes and they were wrongly merged into one.** The carrot curve is flat
(-1/day) and is genuine frugality. The farmer curve is **-21/day then -552/day** — a **cliff, not a
decay**, and a policy converged on inaction cannot produce a cliff, because its own inaction is the
dominant driver of its own cash and inaction is smooth. Something *happens* around day 10-15.
Decomposing that window is item 1 below and nothing else should start before it.

### What it did learn, measured

- `water/plant` **2.0-2.4** — it waters roughly twice per planting. A real sequential relationship.
- `harv/plant` **0.49 -> 1.49** and still rising, on boards built from bare tiles.
- `hire` **self-corrected 0.22 -> 0.10** after its prior was freed. PPO undid a bad change unaided.
- `bank_med` $922 -> $2,870 — but see the metric warning below.

### What it did not, and is actively unlearning

`land` 0.0014 -> 0.0006, `animals` 0.0048 -> 0.0012, `feed` 0.03%, `care` 0.20%.
An animal is a *certain* loss unless PLACE, PICKUP and FEED all follow — it escapes after two unfed
nights, first yield is 4-8 days out — so every prefix of the chain is punished and PPO cannot find a
five-step sequence whose every initial segment loses money.

## Metric warnings, learn these before reading any log

- **`money_med` is not an outcome.** It samples `farm.money` when the update happens to end, with
  every game at a different point in its season. It rose $922 -> $2,600 overnight while a replay of
  the same checkpoint ended every game on **$0**. Both honest: the policy learnt to go broke later.
  Use **`bank_final_med`** (`END $x` in the log), the median bank of games that actually *finished*.
- **`ev` is not `ev_signal`.** Terminal rows are ~0.1% of the batch, so `ev` over all rows is mostly
  the critic predicting its own bootstrapped values. `ev` sits at 0.79; `ev_signal` oscillates
  **0.2-0.7 around ~0.4** and is the one the 0.5 kill criterion was written about. Do not quote an
  endpoint — an `ev_signal` of 0.777 was read off one lucky update.
- **`ev_signal` reads n/a when every finished game had the same outcome.** That was 46% of updates
  before the pool and day-0 fixes, now **20%**. The gaps are the information.
- **Day-0 results arrive in a burst.** All 200 day-0 slots start at turn 0 together and terminate
  together, so `games_day0` jumps ~46 -> 216 in one update and cumulative rates are frozen and
  misleading in between. **Unfixed.** The fix is to phase the *initial* day-0 state while keeping
  the restart target at turn 0 — `staggered_states` and `virgin` exist for exactly this.

**Plot before concluding:** `python3 scripts/plot_run.py <run>/log.jsonl -o out.png`.
It differences the cumulative win rates into windowed ones and plots `ev_signal` with its gaps.
Every wrong call in this project came from reading a level off a tail.

## Capacity: answered, and it is not the constraint

BC on a clean supervised target, same data, 3 epochs (`scripts/train_bc.py --d-model N`):

| d_model | params | train loss | ACTED acc |
|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 215,375 | 2.2605 | 0.401 |
| **128 (current)** | **724,879** | **2.0204** | **0.450** |
| 256 | 2,628,623 | 1.8778 | 0.476 |

Diminishing (+0.049 then +0.026) but not exhausted, so 727k is not a hard ceiling.

**The decisive argument is separate and stronger:** `feed` and `care` *declined* over training
(1.35% -> 0.26%, 1.25% -> 0.50%). A net too small to represent FEED would never pick it up — it
would not **unlearn** it over 1,100 updates. Active decay on specific rare, money-correlated ops,
while `water/plant` learns a clean ratio with the same weights, is a credit-assignment signature,
not a representational one. **Deprioritise architecture work.**

What would overturn that: BC accuracy broken down **by op class**. If d_model 256 leaves
feed/care/collfert flat while movement and water climb, the small net genuinely cannot represent
that boundary even handed the label. Only the aggregate was measured.

### The chain rung: built, committed, deliberately NOT deployed

`curriculum.chain_states()`, reached as `--curriculum-mix length:-1:0.15,0:0.5,...`.
A half-built farm at **day 25**: animals placed and fed, wheat in the shed, crops planted, watered
and carrying yield, money left at the full $3,000. Episode is ~120 turns, so the only thing that
fits in the window is the last link — feed what you have, harvest what is in the ground, sell.
Verified: `day -1: $3,000`, 32-38 outstanding jobs, 119 turns left.

**Two reasons it is held.** It is *state-gifting*, not horizon-shortening — unlike `commit.py`,
which shortened decision points without assuming competence the policy never had — and this docs
set already predicts a seeded position decays in 2-3 days unmaintained. So it may relocate the
failure rather than shorten the chain. And adding a 7th rung on top of a curriculum whose dominant
rung was rewritten hours ago reproduces the exact confound that made this investigation hard.

**Deploy only with a no-op control alongside**: run the identical rung with a do-nothing policy and
compare the first 3-5 days. If the trained policy is indistinguishable — feed never fires before the
seeded wheat is gone, no harvest completes — it coasted, whatever the bank says.

**Better follow-up than the chain rung:** build resume points from the policy's **own reachable
states** (sample just before a natural payoff — animals hungry with wheat present, crop ripe), and
walk the resume point earlier as that segment is mastered. Structural rather than authored, so it
stays the right side of the shape/policy line, and decay then diagnoses the policy's own
maintenance failure rather than a mismatch with a hand-built ideal.

## Submission timing, measured

Forward pass **5.7 ms** on 2 cores — 175x headroom at 1 s/turn, and a 719-turn season is 4.1 s
against a 60 s budget. Attention is not our problem.
**But the first call costs 2.44 s of JIT compile**, which lands on turn 0 and would time out.
Warm the JIT at agent construction, or export to numpy. Unmeasured: `adapter.state_from_obs`
dict->State per turn, which a submission must do every turn and which could dominate 5.7 ms.
