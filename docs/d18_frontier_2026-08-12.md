# Is the d18 plan near the frontier, or well short of it?

**Verdict: near the frontier.** The shipped greedy covers 66 of 67 offered jobs on d18.
The true optimum on this exact instance is 67/67 -- proven, not estimated -- and the gap traces to
one specific, well-understood knob (`MAKE_ROOM_HANDS=3`), not to the greedy paradigm itself.

## Method note, read this first

No OR-tools model was built and no from-scratch LNS was needed. The coverage objective `cover.cover`
actually optimises has a trivial ceiling: **you cannot cover more jobs than exist**, and this instance
has 67. So a constructive assignment that covers all 67 jobs, verified against every real constraint
(per-unit turn budget, priced by `cover.py`'s own `materialise`+`turns`; the FERTILIZER supply cap),
is not a "best found in N seconds" bound -- it is a **certificate of optimality**, regardless of how it
was found. That is what section 3 below is: a feasible witness for 67/67, constructed by re-running
`cover.cover()` -- the exact shipped function, unmodified -- with one of its own existing tunables
(`MAKE_ROOM_HANDS`) turned up. No planner source was edited. Total wall time for every experiment
below: under one minute, mostly `kaggle_environments` startup.

## 1. The instance

Captured by monkeypatching `kagg.search.cover.plan` to record its own arguments on day 18 of a
seed-11 run, agent `schedule_agent_1455mh`, against tape `victor`
(`data/seed_scripts/victor/schedule.json`), then raising to stop the run immediately after capture.
`ACTIVE_UNTIL_DAY = 8` in `kagg/search/crew.py`, so day 18 goes through plain `cover.cover`, not the
crew-specific planner.

- **67 jobs**, **12 units**, all starting from one of four hub tiles: `(4,4)`, `(5,4)`, `(4,5)`,
  `(5,5)`.
- **Budget**: 11 units at 23 turns, 1 unit (`u0`) at 24. Total 277 unit-turns.
- **Depots**: `(4,4)`, `(5,4)`, `(4,5)`.
- **Carry**: every unit starts the day's plan holding nothing.
- **Supply cap**: `FERTILIZER: 10`, `WHEAT: 28`, `STRAWBERRY: 18`, `MILK: 6`, `COW: 1`, `SHEEP: 1`,
  rest 0.
- **Wanted (today's sell row)**: `STRAWBERRY: 37`, `MILK: 21`.
- **`rush=False`, `deliver=False`, `due=None`** -- confirmed these exact captured values were used in
  every experiment below, not defaults.
- Job mix by op: `WATER x48, HARVEST x13, FEED x12, CARE x12, COLLECT_FERTILIZER x12, FERTILIZE x10,
  PLANT x1`. Job cost (turns) histogram: `{1: 48, 2: 5, 3: 5, 4: 9}`, summing to 109 turns of pure
  work before any travel.
- Total FERTILIZER demand across all 67 jobs is exactly **10** -- exactly the supply cap. Every
  FERTILIZE job matters; there is no slack in that resource.

**The one uncovered job**: `Job(0, 0)['WATER', 'FERTILIZE', 'HARVEST']` -- 3 turns of work, needs 1
FERTILIZER, `at_risk=True` (a tile that weeds tonight if skipped again). It sits in the far corner,
Manhattan distance 8 from the nearest depot.

Checked whether this is a hard capacity bind before assuming it is a search failure: summed
FERTILIZER demand across the 66 *covered* jobs is 9, against a cap of 10 -- one unit of fertilizer
was sitting unused. Not a supply bind.

Checked geometric feasibility: the cheapest possible insertion of this job into any single unit's
*existing* route, with that unit's turn budget ignored entirely, is 24 turns (on `u9`) -- one turn
over `u9`'s actual budget of 23. Every other unit's unconstrained-insertion cost is higher still. So
no single-hand insertion fits, which is exactly why the shipped `MAKE_ROOM` repair pass (which tries
to free room on one hand at a time) was invoked and still failed.

## 3. Offline result: proven 67/67

`cover.py` already carries the tunables that gate its own repair search:

```
MAKE_ROOM_HANDS = 3   # how many candidate hands MAKE_ROOM tries, cheapest-to-reach first
MAKE_ROOM_MOVES = 8   # how many stops may be shed off one hand while trying
```

Re-running the *unmodified* `cover.cover()` on the identical captured instance (same units, jobs,
budget, depots, carry, supply, wanted, day, rush, `deliver=False` -- the real captured value, checked
explicitly, see below) with `MAKE_ROOM_HANDS` swept from 3 to 12, `MAKE_ROOM_MOVES` fixed at 8:

```
HANDS=3   -> covered 66/67
HANDS=4   -> covered 66/67
HANDS=5   -> covered 66/67
HANDS=6   -> covered 66/67
HANDS=7   -> covered 66/67
HANDS=8   -> covered 67/67   <- flips here
HANDS=9..12 -> covered 67/67
```

`MAKE_ROOM_MOVES` was not the limiting factor -- sweeping it to 40 with `HANDS` still at 3 makes no
difference. The fix requires `MAKE_ROOM_HANDS >= 8`.

**Root cause**: `MAKE_ROOM_HANDS=3` restricts the repair pass to the 3 units *cheapest to insert the
stuck job into directly*. The unit that can actually make room for it, `u3`, ranks **8th** by that
metric (raw insertion cost is high because `u3` isn't near the corner) -- but `u3` is carrying jobs
that are cheap to relocate elsewhere. The fix found is a genuine 3-way chain: `u3` sheds 6 stops
(`(6,4) (7,0) (7,1) (7,2) (7,4) (8,0)`), 2 of them land on `u1` and 4 on `u9` (both of which had
slack), which drops `u3` from 23 turns down to 14 and leaves it free to take the corner job alone at
a total cost of 14 -- cheaper than any of the direct-insertion costs computed for the other 11 units.
"Cheapest to reach" and "cheapest to clear a path for" are different rankings, and the shipped cap
only searches the first one.

Re-verified with the actual captured `deliver=False` (not assumed `True`): still 67/67. `deliver` is
in fact accepted but unused inside `cover()` itself -- grep confirms the only occurrence of the word
`deliver` between `def cover(` and `def from_nodes(` is the signature line; `cover()`'s own `cost()`
closure hardcodes `materialise(..., True/False, ...)` from `RESERVE_THE_TRIP_FOR_TODAYS_HARVEST`
regardless of the caller's `deliver` argument. So this did not confound the result either way, and
was checked rather than assumed.

**Constraint compliance of the 67/67 solution**, checked explicitly, not assumed from `cover()`
returning `left=[]`:

FERTILIZER used: 10 / cap 10. All within budget, all within supply. Total packed cost rose from 235
to 244 (9 turns) to cover the 67th job -- three for the job's own ops, the rest travel and the
fertilizer pickup, absorbed entirely out of the 42 turns of existing slack.

## 4. Cost-model validation

Per project rule, verified the cost model reproduces `cover`'s own priced cost for at least two
tours before trusting anything above. No independent cost model was written -- every number above
comes from calling `cover.py`'s own `materialise()` and `turns()` directly, so this is fidelity by
construction rather than by approximation, and was still checked explicitly rather than assumed:

```
u2:  cover's own cost=23   recomputed=23   match=True
u11: cover's own cost=23   recomputed=23   match=True
```

(`u2` and `u11` are the two units the shipped greedy ran flush to their budget on, the tightest
tours available to check against.)

## 5. Gap

| | jobs covered | of | uncovered |
|---|---|---|---|
| greedy (shipped, `MAKE_ROOM_HANDS=3`) | 66 | 67 | `Job(0,0)[WATER,FERTILIZE,HARVEST]` |
| offline (proven optimum) | 67 | 67 | none |

**Gap: 1 job (1.5% of the day's job list), and it is not a hard capacity bind.** It costs a specific,
already-named constant (`MAKE_ROOM_HANDS`, `kagg/search/cover.py`) being tuned for repair-pass
runtime rather than search depth -- the file's own comment on it notes the pass is
`O(jobs x hands x stops x moves)`, which is presumably why it ships narrow. Whether `MAKE_ROOM_HANDS`
can be raised within the 1s `actTimeout` is a separate question this measurement does not answer and
no planner code was changed to find out.

## Verdict

**The planner is near the frontier on d18.** The gap measured here is one job, traceable to a single
named, already-tunable constant, not to the greedy-with-repair paradigm being structurally incapable
of the instance. This does not by itself explain the full unit-turns/idle gap against Victor's tape
noted in the handoff (275 ours vs 272 his, more of ours spent moving) -- that is a tour-shape /
schedule question, a different lever than job-count coverage, and outside what this measurement
covers.
