# The plan: 6 tapes, 5 schedules, one fluid layer

Every number here is measured on **179 seasons of SpaTaro's submission 56114097** and nothing else.
Mixing submissions is what made the earlier 62-season set useless.
Rows come from `data/spataro_landed_rows.json` — what his board actually DID, turn to turn — not from his orders: he over-requests `BUY_LAND` in 162 of 179 seasons and funds exactly **two parcels in all 179**.

The basis is fixed. The variation on top of it is the point.

## Layer 3 — five schedules, days 9–29

**There is no second split to fit.** 60 distinct (first shop, second shop) pairs over 179 seasons, p50 3 seasons per pair, **zero pairs with 8 or more**. Keying a tape on the shop pair fits noise.

## Layer 4 — the fluid rule, every day

The layer that cannot come from a recording, because his `plants`/`seeds`/`buys`/`sells`/`hires` are **179 distinct plans from day 4** — a continuous function of money and prices, with nothing to copy.

Three live decisions, each with its mechanism named:

1. **Fill.** Plant every open tile with the cluster's crop until seeds or worker-turns run out. His `planted` is p50 61 of 75 tiles at d12 and his `buys` is WHEAT every single day (31/44/21/4/13/20/13/24/16/55 on d0–9). Wheat in, milk and wool out — the 83%-of-revenue finding in REGISTER OPEN-3.
2. **Stop feeding at the season boundary.** `DIG` cannot remove a placed animal; `consecutive_unfed >= 2` escapes it and leaves the structure (`reference/kaggriculture.py:817`). Measured across the 179: 369 COW + 693 SHEEP escapes, and they are **concentrated at the very end** — 216 on d27 and 472 on d28, against 26–71 per day before that. His sheep herd PEAKS at day 27 (peak p50 7, peak day p50 27) and ends at p50 2.

   So this is not a market-crash cull. He buys sheep late, collects yield to the end, and stops feeding when there is no yield left to collect — which costs nothing, because an animal has no exit value (`DIG` cannot remove it, it cannot be sold). It is free money at the boundary. The earlier "217 -> 11, cull day p50 26" reading came from the mixed-submission 62-season set and does NOT survive on his current agent; mid-season escapes run under one per season per day.

   The rule this implies is small and safe: stop `feeds` for animals whose next yield falls after day 29. Nothing else.
3. **Refill.** Carrot into the tiles freed as melon and strawberry come out. Carrot is 0 at d12 in seven of eight branches, appears at d15 (p75 12), and by d26 is in EVERY cluster (2/18/11/10/23 for milk_berry/wheat/berry/wool/carrot) — the late refill is universal and only its size is world-dependent. "Fill the gaps with wheat or carrot" is two phases: wheat through the middle, carrot from ~d20.

Ridge and not a tree or a net: 179 seasons against nine features is a linear problem, and anything with more capacity fits the sample.

**So: ridge for the p50 target, the cluster matrix for the p25/p75 tolerance band.** Ridge gives a point estimate; the band is what tells the planner when to do nothing. Inside p25–p75, no action. Outside, close the gap.

## Level 4, first rule: stop feeding what can no longer pay

`kagg/search/dayplan.py`, in the pen task builder. A pen whose next production day falls past
`LAST_DAY` gets `["COLLECT_FERTILIZER"]` instead of `["FEED", "CARE", "COLLECT_FERTILIZER"]`.

The mechanism, from the engine rather than from tuning: `_daily_refresh_animals` credits a yield for
`next_day = day + 1` (`reference/kaggriculture.py:805`), so a FEED on day D can only produce the unit
credited on D+1, and there is no day after 29. Feeding past that point costs 1 WHEAT per animal per
day and returns nothing -- and it is the same wheat the field wants.

This is the whole of SpaTaro's "cull", and the earlier reading of it was wrong. Over 179 seasons his
escapes are 216 on d27 and 472 on d28 against 26-71 a day before that, his sheep herd PEAKS at d27
(p50), and **zero of the 179 seasons dip mid-season and rebuild**. He is not culling into a crashed
market. He stops paying for an animal at the point it can no longer pay him, which costs nothing
because `DIG` cannot remove a placed animal and it cannot be sold.

HARVEST stays in the ops deliberately: the escape replaces the whole tile, so any `yield_units` still
standing are lost with it, and the two days before the animal goes are exactly when they must be
collected.
